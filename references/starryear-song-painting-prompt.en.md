# 【S.009】Starryear-Song-Painting | Full Prompt

Treat the user's single uploaded photograph as the sole content evidence, compositional basis, and color source. Create one continuous vertical editorial artwork that descends through “photographic evidence → painterly reconstruction → minimalist afterimage.” Do not apply one filter to all three stages or invent a generic East Asian scene unrelated to the photograph.

## 1. Role of the input image

There is one source photograph. The upper stage must use the photograph itself. Permit only proportional scaling and a slight crop needed for the horizontal window while preserving the principal subject and directional relationship. Do not redraw, recolor, retouch, smooth, erase or add objects, outpaint, replace the background, or use generative fill. The middle and lower stages may reconstruct the image, but every important shape, direction, rhythm, area of negative space, and color must trace back to the photograph.

If the source is not horizontal, preserve the decisive subject and main direction through a window crop without stretching. When essential content cannot survive a horizontal crop, modestly reduce the window height instead of damaging the photograph.

## 2. Internal observation and translation

Perform the following analysis internally and do not output it:

1. Identify three to six decisive facts: the main structural line, subject anchor, repeated small forms, background color field, dense cluster, and largest area of negative space.
2. Map them into a source-bound grammar shared by all stages: the same main direction, relative placement, rhythm family, and accent color.
3. Preserve full real-world information above; remove photographic micro-detail while retaining the spatial skeleton and recognizable clues in the middle; keep only one essential structural action, a few echoes, and one accent below.
4. Make information density decrease in one direction, from witnessing the scene to a material memory and finally to quiet testimony.

## 3. Three-stage structure

### A. Upper stage: photographic evidence

- Place it in a warm ivory editorial field at the top, occupying about 30% of total height.
- Set the photograph in a horizontal window with side safety margins of about 5%–7% of canvas width and a photo width of about 88%–90%.
- Above it, left aligned, add one small sans-serif label: `SOURCE 01 / ORIGINAL`; the number may change with the series.
- Keep the label restrained, dark gray, and slightly letter-spaced; never place it over the photo.
- This stage must read immediately as unaltered source evidence.

### B. Middle stage: surreal reconstruction through Song spatial logic

- Occupy about 31%–34% of total height and run nearly full bleed horizontally, joining adjacent regions with clean boundaries.
- Extend the photograph's dominant background color into a matte field resembling aged paper, mineral pigment, pale rubbing, or thin wax.
- Use the awareness of high distance, deep distance, level distance, and mobile viewpoints associated with Northern Song landscape painting, without copying any specific historical work or automatically adding conventional landscape symbols. Keep the source direction and center of gravity traceable.
- Do not merely copy the photograph under an ink or printmaking filter. Change at least two of scale, occlusion, distance, hierarchy, and continuity. Make dominant masses taller, more isolated, and more architectural, producing a space clearly different from the photographic site.
- Rebuild the source structure through concentrated ink, accumulated ink, broken ink, pale wash, axe-cut rock texture, dry brush, flying-white gaps, rubbing, erasure, bleeding, and thin mineral color. Allow discontinuous perspective, suspended planes, floating rock shelves, passage through mist, broken horizons, or disappearing paths.
- Select one line, edge, or color block that truly exists in the photograph and turn it into an impossible connector. It may pass through, circle, disappear into, or reappear between dominant masses, but its color, direction, and rhythm must come from the source.
- Translate repeated small forms into hand stamps, pale rubbings, or incomplete contours. Derive their number and clustering from the source; simplification is allowed, spontaneous multiplication is not.
- Reduce and embed the subject anchor within the structure while keeping minimum necessary recognizability. For a person or animal, preserve pose, orientation, and key identifying cues, with no extra faces, eyes, or limbs. For architecture or objects, preserve the defining contour and relative scale.
- Make this stage more spatially estranged and tactile, like Song spatial logic reorganized through contemporary editorial mixed media. It must remain traceable to the same scene without reading as a direct copy.
- A dark transition band measuring 1%–2% of canvas height may appear at the bottom, using the source's darkest structural color.

### C. Lower stage: minimalist afterimage

- Occupy about 34%–37% of total height and return to broad warm-ivory negative space.
- Center a horizontal paper work inside a pale, fine double frame. Set the outer frame to about 82% of canvas width and the inner artwork to about 70%, leaving obvious mounting space.
- Distill the main source structure into one dominant arc, angle, diagonal, mass, or contour, executed with one to three dark strokes whose wetness varies. Do not retell the entire scene.
- Extract three to seven pale echoes from repeated source forms, rendered in white, off-white, or a very pale source color. They are mnemonic traces, not realistic icons.
- Compress the subject anchor into one small solid dot, leaf-like mark, seal-like shape, or short form using the sole source-derived accent color. Keep it near a position consistent with the original relationship.
- Permit only faint abrasion, paper fiber, dry powder, old-print residue, or edge wear. Keep at least 60% of the inner artwork quiet, open, and low contrast.
- Below the frame, set a small issue/date line at left, such as `NO. 001   04 SEP 2026`, and a 2–4-word uppercase English title at right. Derive the title from the photograph's mood and action; do not literally name its species or location and do not write grandiose poetry.

## 4. Whole canvas and joins

- Fix the output as one vertical image at an 8:15 ratio; use 1600 × 3000 px or a proportionally larger resolution.
- Use a low-saturation warm ivory background, never pure white.
- Do not make an equal-panel grid. The top is editorial photographic evidence, the middle is a nearly full-bleed high-density material field, and the bottom is a mounted low-density afterimage.
- Preserve the strict top-to-bottom reading order. Do not swap stages, arrange them horizontally, or return three separate images.
- Density, midtone complexity, and literal representation must decrease downward while source-derived structural relationships remain visible.

## 5. Color system

Extract color from the photograph instead of imposing the reference image's blossom palette:

- One background dominant: from the largest source color field, desaturated and translated into paper.
- One dark structural color: from the darkest branch, contour, shadow, or boundary.
- One accent color: from the subject or the smallest highly identifying source color, used sparingly only for the middle anchor and one lower mark.
- Zero to two pale echo colors: from repeated small forms or highlights.

Do not introduce unrelated saturated colors. Keep the whole piece warm, matte, and low-reflective. The accent may be distinct but must occupy very little area.

## 6. Marks, materials, and surface

Allow cotton or washi-like fibers, mild aged-paper variation, dry brush, ink, charcoal dust, rubbing, stamping, erasure, scraping, pale bleeding, and extremely subtle registration drift.

Prohibit a uniform oil-paint filter, heavy digital noise, plastic surfaces, glow, lens flare, strong gradients, dramatic drop shadows, 3D embossing, perspective mockup frames, obvious AI smearing, gibberish, duplicated subjects, and unsupported pavilions, trees, flora, fauna, moons, fantasy creatures, calligraphy, or seals.

Paper and ink must serve the gradual quieting of information, never conceal structural errors with dirt.

## 7. Typography

Allow text in only three places: the upper source label, lower-left issue/date, and lower-right English title. Use a clean modern sans serif in dark gray or the dark structural color, at restrained sizes with modest letter spacing.

The English title must contain 2–4 uppercase words, be newly derived from the current photograph, and evoke quiet ideas such as witness, pause, echo, wind, distance, or home without copying those examples automatically. Prohibit Chinese calligraphy, paragraphs, verse, signatures, logos, watermarks, QR codes, and extra numbers.

## 8. Adaptive subject handling

- Plants or branches: turn the primary stem into the structural line, foliage or blossoms into repeated echoes, and a small organism into a possible accent anchor.
- People or animals: preserve pose and gaze direction, embed minimum recognizability in the middle, and reduce the figure to one colored point or contour mark below; avoid portrait distortion.
- Architecture or cities: use dominant edges and perspective axes as structural actions and windows, lights, or tiles as repeated echoes; do not change architectural identity.
- Landscapes, coasts, or clouds: turn horizons, ridges, water, roads, railings, or cloud bands into suspended planes, monumental rock structures, disappearing paths, or impossible connectors; keep boats, rocks, and lights as tiny scale anchors.
- Still life: derive structure from the support plane and object contours, and derive repeated patterns only from actual vessels, fruit, folds, or shadows.

## 9. Adjustable parameters and invariants

Adjustable: source number, date, English title, upper window height, main-stroke family, middle paper medium, lower echo count, and accent intensity.

Invariants: one 8:15 vertical image; the fixed order of photograph, painterly reconstruction, and minimalist afterimage; no content alteration in the upper photo; both transformed stages derive from the same source; density decreases downward; only one small-area accent color; exactly three typography zones; and one finished output.

## 10. Output limits

Return exactly one completed 8:15 vertical poster image. Do not return analysis, prompts, explanations, alternatives, storyboards, comparison sheets, or three separated panels. Exclude full-photo filtering, unrelated stages, a middle stage that copies the photograph, generic pseudo-antique landscapes, unsupported pavilions or trees, a photorealistic middle stage, a lower stage so empty that it loses source linkage, ornate decoration, incorrect text, crooked framing, and all generation artifacts.
